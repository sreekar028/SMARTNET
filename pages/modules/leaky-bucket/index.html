Leaky Bucket Algorithm — Theory & Mathematical Formulation

Understanding Traffic Shaping vs. Traffic Policing in Computer Networks

1. Mathematical Model

At any discrete time step \(t\), let \(A_t\) be the incoming burst of packets, \(B\) be the finite bucket buffer capacity, and \(L\) be the constant leak/outflow rate:

Dropped Packets:   D_t = max(0,   Q_t + A_t - B)
Buffer Level:   Q_{t+1} = max(0,   min(B, Q_t + A_t) - L)

Any incoming packet arriving when \(Q_t + A_t > B\) exceeds the buffer capacity and is immediately dropped (packet loss due to buffer exhaustion).

2. Leaky Bucket vs. Token Bucket

  • Leaky Bucket: Enforces a strictly constant output rate regardless of incoming traffic burstiness. Ideal for ATM networks and jitter-sensitive applications.
  • Token Bucket: Collects tokens at a steady rate. Allows bursty transmission as long as accumulated tokens are available.
  • Traffic Policing: Drops or marks non-conforming packets immediately without buffering.
  • Traffic Shaping: Buffers bursty packets and delays them to output a smooth, conforming stream (implemented by Leaky Bucket).
Role in SMARTNET: Implemented at intermediate routers (e.g., \(R_1, R_2, R_3\)) to regulate access links from hosts (\(PC_1\)–\(PC_4\)) to the high-speed server uplink, mitigating packet drops and preventing core switch buffer bloat.
